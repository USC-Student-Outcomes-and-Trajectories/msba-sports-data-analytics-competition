# Official Competition Rulebook: MSBA Sports Data Analytics Competition

## 📜 1. Introduction & Objectives
The **MSBA Sports Data Analytics Competition** is organized by the University of Southern California (USC) Marshall School of Business. It is designed to challenge students to apply cutting-edge data science, statistical modeling, and interactive visualization to real-world basketball problems. 

The prompt is open-ended and focused on strategic problem-solving. Competitors will analyze provided basketball datasets to develop a live, interactive analytics solution (a single dashboard or graphic) that delivers intuitive, actionable insights for coaches and basketball executives during a live game.

---

## 👥 2. Roster Rules & Eligibility
* **Student Status:** Open to active graduate students (MSBA, MBA, Data Science, or related quantitative programs) enrolled at invited universities.
* **Team Size:** Exactly **4 to 5 students** per team.
* **School Cap:** Invited universities are permitted to nominate a **maximum of 2 teams** to represent their institution.
* **Roster Lock:** Roster nominations are locked on **December 18, 2026 (11:59 PM PST)**. After this date, rosters are locked, and substitutions are only allowed under extreme extenuating circumstances approved by the organizing committee.

---

## 📂 3. Submission Deliverables & Specifications
To reflect real-world executive workflows, the deliverable is **not** a static presentation slide deck. Submissions consist of a **live interactive web tool** and an open **code repository**:

### A. Live Interactive Dashboard / Visualization
* **Format:** A public, accessible web link to a running, interactive application (e.g., hosted on Streamlit Community Cloud, Shiny Server, Vercel, Render, Dash, Observable, or Tableau Public).
* **Interactivity & Scenario Selection:** The tool must be self-explanatory and provide judges with the interactive ability to select a specific game, time, or game situation to evaluate the relevance of the analytics in context.
* **Visual Clarity:** Must function as a single dashboard or live visual solution that a coach or executive can understand within seconds during a game.
* **Data Restriction:** The tool must **exclusively use the data provided** by the competition committee. External dataset blending or scraping is strictly prohibited.
* **Anonymity Rule (CRITICAL):** For Round 1 blind evaluation, the live dashboard and interface must contain **NO references to school names, school colors, school logos, or student names**. Submissions containing identifying marks will face immediate point deductions.

### B. Technical Code Repository Requirements
* **Hosting:** Public GitHub repository (or private repository shared with committee judging accounts).
* **Reproducibility:** The codebase must contain:
  1. A `README.md` including the live demo URL, an executive overview of the strategic problem addressed, methodological breakdown, and execution instructions.
  2. A dependency manifest (e.g., `requirements.txt`, `environment.yml`, `package.json`, or Dockerfile).
  3. Clean, well-documented code that runs reliably.
  4. Disclosure of any AI tools used during the project lifecycle.

## ⚖️ 5. Evaluation Criteria & AI Policy

### Core Judging Criteria
Judges evaluate submissions on four core pillars:
1. **Clarity of Visual (30%):** Ease of interpretability and instant understanding. Can an executive or coach glance at the display and immediately grasp the game state?
2. **Actionability (35%):** Ability to directly translate into an immediate, strategic decision (e.g., substitution timing, defensive scheme adjustment, foul risk mitigation).
3. **Feasibility (20%):** Practical operational viability. Can this decision realistically be enacted during an actual game situation?
4. **Technical Rigor & Defense (15%):** Engineering quality of the codebase, mathematical validity of the underlying logic, and the team's ability to defend their model in Q&A.

### Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy
* **Allowed & Encouraged:** Students are **fully allowed and encouraged to use modern AI tools** (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Cursor, generative UI assistants).
* **Evaluation Focus:** Teams are judged on the **tools they create, how intuitive and clear those tools are, and how convincingly they can explain, justify, and defend their design decisions and analytical framework** when interacting with judges. AI is a productivity accelerator, not a substitute for rigorous domain reasoning.
